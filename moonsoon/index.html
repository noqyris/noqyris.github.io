Questions about Moonsoon? Start here — the same answers are in the app, under Settings → Help & support.

Getting started

How do I track a night?

Before bed, tap Start Sleep on Home. Moonsoon listens through the night and saves the sounds it picks up. You can lock your phone — recording keeps going.

In the morning, tap I am awake. That stops tracking, saves the night and opens its report.

iOS doesn't let apps start recording by themselves, so every night starts with that one tap. A session shorter than 20 minutes isn't saved.

Can I start from Control Center, the Lock Screen or the Action Button?

Yes. Moonsoon has a Start Sleep widget for your Home Screen, StandBy and Lock Screen, and a Start Sleep control for Control Center, the Lock Screen's buttons and the Action Button. Each one opens Moonsoon and starts your night, just like tapping Start Sleep on Home.

Widget: touch and hold an empty spot on your Home Screen, choose Add Widget and search for Moonsoon. For the Lock Screen, touch and hold it, tap Customize, then the widget area. Widgets need iOS 16.2 or later.

Control: open Control Center, tap +, then Add a Control, and search for Moonsoon. Controls need iOS 18 or later.

Action Button: in the Settings app, open Action Button, choose Controls or Shortcut, and pick Moonsoon's Start Sleep. You can also ask Siri to "start sleep tracking in Moonsoon".

iOS doesn't let apps start recording by themselves, so each of these opens Moonsoon first — from a locked phone, after Face ID or your passcode.

Can Sleep Focus remind me to start my night?

Yes, on iOS 17.2 and later. First turn on Settings → Sleep Focus reminder in Moonsoon — the reminder never comes without it. Then add Moonsoon's Focus filter: in the Settings app, open Focus, then Sleep. Under Focus Filters, tap Add Filter, choose Moonsoon, keep Remind me on and tap Add. When Sleep Focus turns on, Moonsoon sends one reminder — “Ready for bed?” — and a tap on it starts your night.

It comes only for a Sleep Focus that turns on in the evening or at night (never for a nap during the day), never while you wind down or a night is being tracked, and at most once a night. It needs notifications allowed for Moonsoon, and Sleep Focus silences notifications: to see it when it arrives, add Moonsoon to the apps Sleep Focus allows, in the same place. Moonsoon makes it on your device and sends nothing anywhere. To stop it, turn Sleep Focus reminder off.

Prefer your night to start by itself? In the Shortcuts app, make a personal automation that runs Moonsoon's Start sleep tracking when Sleep Focus turns on. How to set it up, under Sleep Focus reminder in Moonsoon's Settings, shows both.

Where should I put my phone?

On your nightstand or bedside table, close to your head, with nothing covering it. The nearer it is to you, the more clearly Moonsoon hears you.

Never put it under your pillow or mattress — especially while it's charging. It can overheat there, and the bedding muffles the very sounds Moonsoon listens for.

Plugging it in is a good idea: listening all night uses battery.

Can Moonsoon track my partner's snoring?

Yes. On Tonight, under Listening to, choose My partner before you tap Start Sleep, and put your device by your partner's side of the bed. Moonsoon remembers the choice for the nights after — choose Me again on a night that's yours.

Ask your partner first. Their night is recorded and analysed on your device like any other, and Moonsoon never uploads it.

Their night's report speaks about them, snoring first: how long they snored and when it was loudest, any sleep talk and breathing pauses (beta). It has no sleep score — a phone by their side hears their snoring well and their sleep poorly. Share this night makes a picture of their snoring; it's their night, so ask before you send it.

Their nights stay out of everything that's yours: your trends and averages, the 7-night average, experiments and findings, the weekly goal, your month in sleep, Siri, the doctor PDF and Apple Health. Moonsoon never turns their words into text, and never adds your Apple Watch's stages or vitals to their night — your Watch measures you, not them.

In All nights and in Trends' Recent nights, their nights carry a Partner badge. On their night, a clip that was really you is marked That's me — don't count it. Trends shows their snoring in a section of its own, Your partner's snoring — their trend, never part of yours.

Can I track a nap?

Yes. Between 10 AM and 6 PM, Start Sleep first asks Nap or night? Choose how long to nap — 20, 30, 45 or 90 minutes — and Moonsoon wakes you that long after the nap begins, with your alarm sound. Start the night starts a normal night instead.

A nap's alarm is its own: your alarm settings and tomorrow's alarm stay as they are. It has the same backup as a night's alarm, and Snooze works as usual. A nap skips your Start listening after delay: listening starts right away — or, if your sleep sound plays, when it stops, as on a night. A sleep sound never plays past the nap's alarm: one longer than the nap stops when the alarm rings, and Moonsoon doesn't listen while it plays.

A nap is saved like a night when it lasts at least 20 minutes. Its report is short: when you napped, your snoring and the sounds Moonsoon heard. It has no sleep score — the score weighs a whole night against your sleep goal.

Naps stay out of everything about your nights: your trends and averages, the 7-night average, experiments and findings, the weekly goal, your month in sleep, your rhythm, the doctor PDF and Siri. Moonsoon doesn't write naps to Apple Health: over a short nap, a microphone can't tell rest from sleep. In All nights, a nap carries a Nap badge.

Can Moonsoon help me wind down before a night?

Yes. On Tonight, switch on Wind down first, then choose 3, 5 or 10 minutes and a pattern. With 4-7-8 you breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 7 and breathe out for 8 — four times, as the method itself suggests — then go on with slow breaths without holding (in for 4, out for 6) for the rest of the time. The gentler 4-4-6 (in for 4, hold for 4, out for 6) goes all the way through. When you tap Start Sleep, a calm screen guides your breathing: the moon grows as you breathe in, stays full while you hold and settles as you breathe out, with a soft tap at each change if you keep haptic cues on. With Reduce Motion on, a count takes the moon's place.

Your night then starts by itself, with your usual sleep sound, alarm, Start listening after delay and choice of whose night it is. Nothing is recorded during the wind-down: the microphone opens only when the night starts. Start my night now skips the rest, and the close button goes back to Tonight without starting anything.

The screen dims while you breathe and stays on, so it doesn't lock before your night starts. To wind down every night, switch on Settings → Sleep → Always wind down first — you can still switch it off on Tonight for one night. Tonight's alarm is already set while you breathe: if the screen is locked before your night starts, nothing is recorded, but the alarm still wakes you.

Breathe gently and never strain. If you feel lightheaded, breathe normally or choose 4-4-6. The wind-down is free for everyone.

What's different about my first night?

Your first tracked night is fully unlocked: you can play back every clip Moonsoon saved, even on the free plan.

From the second night on, the free plan keeps the 5 most significant clips of each night unlocked — snoring, talking and other clear sounds first, not simply the earliest ones. Premium unlocks every clip.

A session under 20 minutes isn't saved, so a quick test doesn't use up your first night.

What does “What brings you here?” change?

It puts what you came for first, and hides nothing. I snore puts your snoring plan at the top of Trends; I just want to sleep better puts Your rhythm there — your week against your sleep goal. I talk in my sleep shows where Show what you said is; iOS asks for speech recognition only when you turn it on.

My partner snores makes your partner's night the default from your next night, as if you chose it on the Tonight tab: ask them first, and put the device on their side of the bed. Their nights stay out of your trends and Apple Health. You can switch back on the Tonight tab anytime.

You can change your answer anytime in Settings → What brings you here?

Can I add my own tags, or a note about a night?

Yes. Under Last night I… on a night’s report, tags for what a dentist or doctor may have prescribed are built in: Mouthguard (fitted), CPAP, Chin strap, Nasal spray, Allergy medicine and Sleeping pill. A tag of your own you’d already made with one of those names became that tag, with all its nights. For anything else, tap Add to make a tag of your own. Give it a name and an icon: it’s tagged on that night and offered on every report after. You can have up to 20, free on every plan.

Trends treats your own tags like the built-in ones: once you’ve tagged enough nights with one, and enough without it, it can show whether it changes your snoring. The PDF for your doctor lists them too. To see whether the mouthguard your dentist fitted is working, start the Mouthguard experiment in Trends: 7 nights with it, measured against your usual ones.

With Suggest tags from Apple Health on in Settings → Apple Health, a night’s report can suggest Exercised, Late caffeine or Alcohol from what you or your apps logged in Apple Health: a workout earlier that day, caffeine after 2 PM or in the 8 hours before the night, a drink from 4 PM. Nothing is tagged until you tap Add; tap Dismiss and the suggestion won’t come back for that night. A tag you add is yours like any other, and Moonsoon never saves what it read — no workouts, times or amounts.

Rename or delete them in Settings → Your tags. A deleted tag comes off Last night I…, Trends and the PDF; the nights you tagged keep their other tags. Deleted one by mistake? A backup from before brings it back with Restore from a backup. Delete all sleep data removes your notes with the nights, but your tags stay until you delete them there.

For anything a tag can’t say, tap Add a note under the tags. A note holds up to 500 characters, you can edit or delete it any time, and it never goes into the picture you share. Moonsoon sends it nowhere; a backup you make in Settings → Your nights includes your notes and your tags. A night you tracked your partner has no tags — they’re for what changes your own snoring — but it can still have a note.

What is my snoring plan, and does the weather affect my snoring?

Your snoring plan is at the top of the experiments in Trends — or at the very top of Trends, if you told Moonsoon you snore. It works only from your own nights: What helps you ranks the experiments you finished and the patterns in your tags, each with the nights it rests on. A pattern in your tags is a hint, not proof, so a tag counts as helping there only for a change Moonsoon suggests, such as side sleeping or a nasal strip. A night Moonsoon heard less than 60% of doesn't count.

Next to try suggests one change for the coming week: sleeping on your side and raising your head first, then a nasal strip, a humidifier, no evening alcohol and an earlier dinner — and a mouthguard only if your dentist fitted one. In the full plan, one your tags point at comes first. Trying it starts a 7-night experiment; skip anything that doesn't suit you. If your nose is often blocked or stuffy at night, tell the plan: a nasal strip and a humidifier move up your list, on the free plan too.

Week by week takes one change a week, each measured against your usual nights, and at the end shows what to keep doing. Your top experiment result and the next change are free; the full plan is Premium. The plan itself never sends a notification — an experiment reminds you only if you switch its reminder on.

If Moonsoon heard breathing pauses on several of your recent nights, or loud snoring on most of them — or you answered yes in Things to mention to your doctor to someone seeing your breathing stop, to waking up gasping or choking, or to feeling very sleepy during the day — the plan says to talk to a doctor first and offers the PDF for your doctor. It suggests everyday changes to test, not a treatment — and Moonsoon can’t diagnose sleep apnea.

Does the weather affect your snoring? Maybe a little, but it’s hard to say. What’s known about weather and snoring is thin and mixed, and the weather outside says little about your bedroom: heating, air conditioning and an open window change the air you breathe at night more than the forecast does. So Moonsoon doesn’t read the weather, and doesn’t guess.

What you can check is your own nights. If a cold, an allergy or dry air leaves your nose blocked, tag the night Blocked nose under Last night I… on its report. Once enough nights are tagged with it, and enough without, Trends can show whether your snoring changes with it. If your bedroom air feels dry, try the Humidifier week in Your snoring plan: 7 nights with a humidifier on, measured against your usual nights.

What is Ask Moonsoon?

Ask Moonsoon answers questions about your own nights: how you slept, what goes with your snoring, what to try next, or a summary of your week to show your doctor. Open it from its card near the top of Trends, or with Ask about this night on a report, and tap a suggested question or type your own.

Apple Intelligence works out the answers on your device. Moonsoon looks up only what a question needs and hands it over: the numbers your reports show (with the sleep stages from your Apple Watch or another wearable, and your Watch's vitals, if you connected Apple Health), your tags, your experiments and your plan — never your notes, and the words from Show what you said only when you ask what you said. Of a night you tracked for your partner, it hands over only their snoring. Moonsoon never sends your nights anywhere, and the conversation isn't saved: it lasts until Moonsoon is swiped away in the app switcher or iOS closes it, and goes at once with the New conversation button at the top.

You can also speak a question: tap the microphone beside the question field. Your words appear in the field as you speak — check them, then send; nothing is sent by itself. Moonsoon turns speech into text with Apple's on-device speech recognition only, never an online service (where your device can't do that for the conversation's language, the microphone says so), and never keeps or sends the sound. The first time, iOS asks to use the microphone and speech recognition. The microphone stops when you stop speaking, after a minute at most, or when you leave the chat or the app — and it never listens while a night or a nap is being tracked, during a wind-down or while the alarm rings. With Read answers aloud (the speaker button at the top), each answer is read aloud with your device's voice as it appears — even in Silent mode, like a video you play — and the reading stops if your headphones disconnect; while VoiceOver is on, VoiceOver reads the answers instead.

It can offer to set your alarm, start an experiment, tag a night or open a screen, but nothing changes until you tap that button. Every number in an answer is checked against your nights, and one that doesn't match is marked. Answers can still be wrong, and they're never medical advice: Moonsoon can't diagnose sleep apnea — a doctor can, and the PDF for your doctor is made for that conversation.

It needs Apple Intelligence: an iPhone 15 Pro or newer, or an iPad with an M1 or A17 Pro chip or newer, on iOS or iPadOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence turned on in Settings. Where Apple Intelligence doesn't answer in your language yet (Polish, for now), Ask Moonsoon talks in English. Where your device can't run it, Trends doesn't show its card.

Ask Moonsoon is part of Premium, and Lifetime includes it.

What's free, and what does Premium add?

Free: tracking every night, your sleep and snore scores, the night timeline, the 5 most significant clips of each night (every clip on your first night), 3 kept clips, basic Trends and 6 sleep sounds.

Premium: every clip of every night, as many kept clips as you like, all 20 sleep sounds, detailed Trends — your snore trend, every insight, and what changes your snoring, measured from the nights you tag — and your full snoring plan.

On the free plan you can also try one Premium sleep sound a night, free.

Is Moonsoon a medical device, and what should I mention to my doctor?

No. Moonsoon is not a medical device, and nothing in it is a diagnosis. It tells you what it heard during the night and how that compares with your other nights.

Loud, regular snoring with pauses, gasping or choking — or feeling very sleepy during the day — is worth talking to a doctor about.

Things to mention to your doctor lists nine things a doctor may want to hear about your snoring and your sleep, in your own words — that someone has seen your breathing stop or pause in your sleep, that you wake up gasping, choking or snorting, that you feel very sleepy during the day, and six more. Answer Yes, No or Not sure, or leave any of them out. It’s free, at the foot of the Share with your doctor card in Trends and in Your snoring plan.

It isn’t a test: nothing is scored or added up, answering no doesn’t rule anything out, and Moonsoon can’t diagnose sleep apnea. A yes to someone seeing your breathing stop, to waking up gasping or choking, or to feeling very sleepy during the day puts Talk to a doctor first at the top of your snoring plan. Everything you answer yes to goes into the PDF for your doctor. If you can, take someone who has watched you sleep to the appointment.

Moonsoon never sends your answers anywhere. They go into a backup you make in Settings → Your nights, never into the spreadsheet. Clear my answers removes them, and so does Delete all sleep data.

Recording & detection

What sounds does Moonsoon pick up?

Snoring, sleep talking, coughing, a crying baby, pets, movement, breathing and other room sounds. Each one is saved as a short clip on the night's report, at the time it happened.

Detection runs entirely on your device, using Apple's built-in sound classifier. Nothing is uploaded to be analysed.

Moonsoon can't tell one person from another, so a partner's snoring counts as yours until you mark it on the report.

Can Moonsoon tell me what I said?

Yes, when your device can do it on its own. If a night has sleep talking, its report offers Show what you said. The first time, iOS asks whether Moonsoon may use speech recognition. Its message there is about speech recognition in general: Moonsoon only ever uses the on-device kind.

The words are made on your device, from the clip itself: your voice is never sent to us, to Apple or anyone else to be turned into text. If your device can't recognise your language on its own, the report says so rather than send your voice online.

Only clips you can play are turned into text; a locked clip needs unlocking first. When the words aren't clear, the report says Mumbling — no clear words instead of guessing.

The text is kept with its night, on your phone, and Moonsoon never sends it anywhere. Like the rest of your nights — and the clips you keep — it's part of your device's own backup (iCloud or your computer, if you back up); every other clip is kept out of it. It stays after the clip's audio is deleted (7 nights on), and it goes when the night's data is deleted — or all of it at once when you turn off Settings → Privacy → Show what you said.

Apart from that backup, words leave your device only if you add them to a picture you share — of a night, of a quote or of your month — or leave them in a video of a night you share, or switch them on for a backup file you make in Settings → Your nights, each after a confirmation that says so. If you said no to speech recognition, you can turn it on in iOS Settings → Moonsoon.

Never on a night you tracked your partner: their words are theirs, so Moonsoon doesn't turn them into text, and that night's report doesn't offer Show what you said.

What is my month in sleep, and where are my sleep-talk quotes?

From the 1st of each month, Moonsoon looks back at the month before in a few calm cards — once it held 5 or more nights of your own. Home offers it for the first 7 days (See my month), and Trends keeps every month that has one under Your month in sleep. With Monthly recap on in Settings → Notifications, one quiet notification says it's ready, on the evening of the 1st or 2nd.

The cards: the nights you tracked; your average sleep score and snoring per hour heard, against the month before; your best night; how your snoring moved through the month; the experiments you finished; the tag that mattered most, when your tagged nights clearly show one; your quote of the month; and a closing card. Only your own nights count — a night you tracked your partner is never part of your month — and only those Moonsoon heard for most of the time you were in bed count in the averages. On the free plan the tag card names the tag — whether it raised or lowered your snoring, and by how much, is part of detailed Trends.

The quote of the month is one you starred, or else the longest thing you said clearly that month — tap Another quote for the next. It only ever comes from words you can read with Show what you said on: your own sleep talking, never another app's voices, never a locked clip's words.

Everything you said clearly is in Sleep-talk quotes — tap All quotes under a night's sleep talking, or open it from Trends. Play a quote while its clip is on your device, share it, or star it as a favourite.

Every card and every quote can be shared as a picture, after you see exactly what it holds; a picture with your words goes only after These words will leave your phone. No picture holds audio or anything from Apple Health. Moonsoon makes the recap on your device and never sends it anywhere.

What is “Your morning in 3”?

Last night in a few short cards: your sleep score with one line about your snoring, the night's loudest moment to play, and what you said — when there are words you can read. Then See the full report.

Open it the morning after from Your morning in 3 on the Last night card of the Tonight tab, or at the top of that night's report. It never opens by itself, and it's only ever about your own latest night — never a partner's, which has no sleep score and no words.

It shows nothing your plan hides: a clip locked on the free plan stays locked there, and your words appear only with Show what you said on.

It heard my fan (or my partner) as snoring. Can I fix that?

Yes. On the night's report, tap ••• next to the clip. Choose That's not snoring, That's my partner — don't count it, or pick the right label under Wrong label? On a night you tracked your partner, the same menu offers That's me — don't count it for a sound that was yours.

Your snore score, snore time and 7-night trend change straight away. To take it back, open the same menu and choose Undo.

You can correct any clip you can play. A locked clip needs unlocking first.

How is my sleep score worked out?

It comes only from what Moonsoon measured: how long you slept compared with your sleep goal (the biggest part), how much of the night was restless — loud sounds that likely woke you — and how much of it you snored. Your goal is 8 hours unless you change it in Settings → Sleep → Sleep goal; a new goal counts from your next night, and nights already saved keep their score.

The light, deep and REM chart on the report is an illustration of a typical night, drawn around your restless moments. A phone microphone can't measure sleep stages, so the chart doesn't count toward the score. If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, the report can show the stages it measured instead — they don't count toward the score either.

Because it's based on sound, the score is a guide to your nights, not a medical measurement.

A night you tracked your partner has no sleep score: a phone by their side hears their snoring well and their sleep poorly, so their report leads with their snoring.

What does my snore score mean?

It's a number from 0 to 100 for how much you snored — lower is better. It grows with how much of the time Moonsoon heard was snoring, across the whole night, and with how loud the snoring was. It's worked out over the time Moonsoon heard, counting at least 2 hours: a night a call cut short is judged on what was heard, and a few minutes heard can't make a night look heavy.

Under 10 reads Quiet, under 25 Light, under 50 Moderate, and anything higher Heavy. On an 8-hour night, about an hour of snoring scores around 25; a whole night of very loud snoring comes close to 100.

Every night you've saved is worked out the same way, so your snore trend always compares like with like. Because it's based on sound, it's a guide to your nights, not a medical measurement.

What are breathing pauses (beta)?

Some reports have a Breathing pauses (beta) card. Each pause is a moment when steady snoring went completely silent for about 10 to 90 seconds, then came back with a gasp, a snort or a sudden louder snore.

Moonsoon flags one only in the middle of steady snoring, only when nothing at all was heard during the silence, and only when the snoring carried on afterwards — so plenty of quiet moments are never flagged. Tap a time to hear what came right after it.

If that clip wasn't you, or wasn't snoring, correct it with ••• under Night sounds and the pause goes away. Undo brings it back.

Moonsoon can't diagnose sleep apnea. If pauses show up often, or you're sleepy during the day, talk to a doctor.

Can Moonsoon use my Apple Watch's sleep stages?

Yes. If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, open the night's report and tap Use Apple Watch stages. Moonsoon reads the stages your Watch recorded for that night from Apple Health and shows them instead of the illustration, marked From Apple Watch — with Snoring by stage, which shows how your snoring split across them.

Wear an Oura ring, a WHOOP, a Garmin, a Withings or an Ultrahuman instead? If its app writes your sleep stages to Apple Health, Moonsoon reads those too, the same way — Use Apple Watch stages brings them in as well — and marks them “From Oura” (or whichever yours is). A night uses one device's stages, the one that recorded most of it, never a mix of two. Other apps' estimates are never used, and vitals come from an Apple Watch only.

With Sync sleep to Apple Health on in Settings, Moonsoon looks for your Watch's stages by itself when you open a report.

Moonsoon uses them only when they cover most of the night. If your Watch ran out of battery or came off partway, the report says it recorded only part of the night and keeps the illustration. To go back to the illustration on a night, tap Use the illustration instead.

If none show up, either your Watch didn't record that night — its stages can take a while to reach Health after you wake — or Moonsoon isn't allowed to read Sleep. Apple doesn't tell apps which, so check in the Health app: your profile → Apps → Moonsoon.

Moonsoon never sends the stages anywhere — they're kept with the night, like the rest of it — and they don't change your sleep score.

A night you tracked your partner never gets your Watch's stages: your Watch measures you, not them.

Can Moonsoon show my Apple Watch's blood oxygen and breathing?

Yes. If you wear an Apple Watch to bed, open the night's report and tap Show Apple Watch vitals. Moonsoon reads from Apple Health what your Watch recorded inside that night — blood oxygen (average, lowest and when), respiratory rate, heart rate and, with iOS 18 and watchOS 11 or later, Breathing disturbances — and shows them under From your Apple Watch, with how many readings each one rests on.

Once you've allowed it — with that button, or by turning on Sync sleep to Apple Health in Settings — Moonsoon looks for them by itself whenever you open a report.

Breathing disturbances are Apple's own measure: Moonsoon shows Apple's number and, when Apple gives one, Apple's own reading of it (elevated or not elevated). Moonsoon adds no thresholds of its own. Apple Watch measures these; Moonsoon only shows them, and they can't diagnose anything — Apple itself intends its Blood Oxygen readings for general fitness and wellness, not medical use. Talk to a doctor if you're concerned.

If none show up, either your Watch didn't record them that night — they can take a while to reach Health after you wake — or Moonsoon isn't allowed to read them. Apple doesn't tell apps which, so check in the Health app: your profile → Apps → Moonsoon.

They're free, like your Watch's stages. Moonsoon never sends them anywhere: they're kept with your nights — so, like them, in your device's own backup — and never go into a Moonsoon backup file or the picture of a night you share. They go into the PDF from Share with your doctor only while Include Apple Watch vitals is on.

A night you tracked your partner never gets your Watch's vitals: your Watch measures you, not them.

Why are there only a few clips — or none?

Moonsoon saves a clip only when a sound stands out from the quiet of your room, so a calm night can have very few.

If you snore quietly, set Listening sensitivity to High in Settings, under Sleep: softer sounds become clips too.

While your sleep sound plays, Moonsoon doesn't record, so it never picks up its own audio. Listening starts when the sound's timer ends — or when your Start listening after delay is over, if that's later.

On a very busy night, Moonsoon saves only the most significant clips. Your snore time still counts every snore it heard.

If the session screen said Recording paused, the microphone was interrupted — by a phone call, for example. Moonsoon tries to restart it by itself, and tapping the message restarts it too.

Can I play a sleep sound while tracking?

Yes. In Sounds, pick a sound and how long it should play. It starts when you tap Start Sleep and stops when its timer ends.

Moonsoon doesn't record while it plays, so the sound never ends up in your clips. Listening starts when it stops — or when your Start listening after delay is over, if that's later.

Changed your mind in the night? While it plays, tap the sound on the night screen: Stop now fades it out, 15 more minutes keep it going, and the slider changes how loud it is. Listening still starts only once it has stopped.

Free sounds: White noise, Pink noise, Brown noise, Gentle rain, Ocean waves and Box fan. Premium unlocks all 20.

On the free plan you can try one Premium sound a night: tap a locked sound to hear its first few seconds, then Try it tonight — free. It's yours for that night, and the next night you can try another.

Can I fall asleep to a podcast or music from another app?

Yes. Start it in its own app, before or after you tap Start Sleep. Moonsoon doesn't stop it, and keeps listening while it plays.

Through the speaker, Moonsoon hears it too. So talking heard while another app plays is marked Another app: it isn't counted as your sleep talking or turned into text, and no breathing pause is looked for meanwhile. Snoring still counts. The report says when it happened.

If a clip marked Another app was really you, tap ••• next to it and choose Sleep talking.

With AirPods or other headphones, your audio stays in your ears at full quality, and Moonsoon keeps listening with the phone's own microphone — so the phone still belongs on the nightstand, near you.

It recorded my fan — or missed my quiet snoring. Can I change what it picks up?

Yes. In Settings, under Sleep, choose Listening sensitivity. Normal is right for most bedrooms.

Low is for a fan, an air conditioner or a CPAP: a sound has to be louder and clearer to become a clip, and quiet sounds Moonsoon can't name don't become clips. High is for quiet snorers in a quiet room: softer sounds become clips too.

The change applies from your next night. To fix a clip from a night already recorded, correct it on the report with •••.

Can Moonsoon wait a while before it starts listening?

Yes. On Tonight, under Start listening after, choose 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes. Moonsoon doesn't record until then, so reading and settling in stay out of your clips. The session screen shows when listening starts.

With a sleep sound as well, listening starts when the later of the two ends.

Nothing is heard during the delay, so Moonsoon never reads it as quiet sleep: your sleep score and your snoring per hour count only the time it listened, no breathing pause is flagged in it, and Wake gently can't ring early before listening starts. Your alarm still rings on time.

Alarm & notifications

How does the alarm work?

Set the time and sound on Home before you tap Start Sleep. Moonsoon plays the alarm itself — with the screen locked, too — starting softly and growing louder over its first minute, and keeps it ringing until you tap Snooze or I am awake, for up to 15 minutes.

Snooze quiets it for as long as you chose under Snooze on Home, then it rings again — up to 3 times a morning; after that only I am awake stops it. The backup alarm moves with each snooze.

Under Repeat, choose Every day, or By weekday to give each day of the week its own time — or no alarm at all on the mornings you sleep in. When you tap Start Sleep, the night takes the time of the morning it ends on; Home always shows tonight's.

Plans changed after Start Sleep? Tap the alarm time at the top of the night screen: Change time moves this night's alarm, and No alarm tonight switches it off until the next night. Your alarm settings on Home stay as they are, and the backup alarm follows the change.

With Wake gently set to 10, 20 or 30 minutes, it can ring up to that much earlier — but only once it hears you stirring: turning over, talking or coughing, not a single noise from outside. On a night you track your partner, it listens for their stirring instead. It never rings later than the time you set. Choose Off to be woken at the time exactly.

It only rings during a tracked night. On nights you don't track, use the Clock app.

Its volume follows your phone's media volume; the slider on Home sets how loud it is within that. Home and the night screen both tell you when it would ring too quietly or into headphones. Switch the alarm off on Home or in Settings → Sleep → Wake-up alarm.

Does silent mode or a Focus stop the alarm?

Not while Moonsoon is tracking. The alarm is sound the app plays, like music, so silent mode and Focus modes such as Sleep don't mute it. Your media volume does, so keep it turned up. On iOS 26 and later, if it's low or headphones are connected at alarm time, Moonsoon stays quiet and lets its system alarm ring instead, if you allowed it.

The backup for a night Moonsoon was closed during depends on your iOS version. On iOS 26 and later it's a system alarm, which rings through silent mode and Focus like the Clock app's. Before that it's a notification marked Time Sensitive, with your alarm's sound and again 2 minutes later: it can come through a Focus if Time Sensitive notifications are allowed for Moonsoon — but with your device on silent it won't make a sound.

My alarm didn't go off. Why?

The alarm plays from the app, so it can only ring while Moonsoon is still running. If it was closed during the night — swiped away in the app switcher, or stopped by iOS — the in-app alarm can't play.

That's what the backup is for. On iOS 26 and later, if you allow it, Moonsoon sets a system alarm for your alarm time, which rings even with the app closed. On earlier versions, or if you didn't allow it, Moonsoon schedules a notification with your alarm's sound for 2 minutes after your alarm time instead, and another 2 minutes after that — they need notifications to be allowed for Moonsoon. Either way the backup only reaches you if the alarm itself didn't ring or couldn't be heard — the notifications also come if the alarm rang its full 15 minutes without anyone stopping it.

If iOS paused Moonsoon and it only resumes more than 15 minutes after your alarm time, it won't ring that late — the session screen tells you the alarm was missed instead.

For a dependable wake-up: keep tracking running (don't swipe Moonsoon away), plug your phone in, and keep your media volume up.

Can Moonsoon nudge me when I snore?

Yes, if you'd like it to. In Settings → Sleep, switch on Nudge me when I snore. After 3 minutes of steady snoring, Moonsoon plays a soft chime — a cue to change position, not an alarm. Tap Hear it to hear how quiet it is. On a night you track your partner, the chime plays by their side, so it nudges them.

If you snore on after a chime, the next one that night is a little louder; if the snoring stopped, the next one stays at the same level. It never goes past a quiet limit, so it stays gentle for someone asleep next to you.

It waits 30 minutes into the night, leaves at least 20 minutes between chimes and plays at most 6 a night. It never plays while your sleep sound does, before listening starts, or close to your alarm. The microphone pauses while it plays, so the chime is never recorded.

The report says how many chimes played and after how many of them the snoring stopped within 2 minutes. It's a comfort feature, not a treatment — if your snoring worries you, talk to a doctor.

What notifications does Moonsoon send?

Only ones scheduled on your phone. There's no account or server behind them, and they never include your recordings.

Reminders and summaries — the bedtime nudge, the weekly and monthly recaps, new patterns and a gentle nudge after a pause — are yours to switch on or off in Settings → Notifications. The heads-up when a night is still recording long after you usually wake is separate: it's on by default and has its own switch there.

Two more come with things you set up: the backup alarm on tracked nights (on iOS 26 and later a system alarm takes its place), and — if you start Premium with an introductory price — a heads-up 2 days before that price ends.

Not getting any? Check that notifications are allowed in iOS Settings → Notifications → Moonsoon.

Privacy & data

Does my audio leave my phone?

Not unless you send it. Recording and sound detection happen on your device, and your clips are stored only in Moonsoon's own storage there. Your nights themselves — their numbers, notes and any words from Show what you said — are part of your device's own backup (iCloud or your computer, if you back up), like any app's data, and so are the clips you keep: a new device restored from that backup has them too. Every other clip is kept out of it.

Moonsoon has no account and no cloud, and never uploads anything itself. Audio leaves only in that backup, for the clips you keep, or if you share a clip or a video of your night yourself, after a confirmation that says so. Apart from that backup, the words Moonsoon read from your sleep talk leave only if you add them to a picture you share — of a night, of a quote or of your month — or leave them in a video of a night you share, or switch them on for a backup file you make — again, each after a confirmation. A support email never includes audio or clips.

If you turn on Apple Health sync, Moonsoon writes each night's time in bed and time asleep to Health — never any audio, and never a night you tracked your partner. If you let it, it also reads the sleep stages and overnight vitals (blood oxygen, respiratory rate, heart rate, breathing disturbances) your Apple Watch recorded for a night. Moonsoon never sends them anywhere: they're kept with your nights, in that same backup, and go into a doctor PDF only if you share one with Include Apple Watch vitals on. With Suggest tags from Apple Health on, it also reads the workouts, caffeine and alcoholic drinks logged before a night, only to suggest its tags, and keeps none of them — only the tags you choose to add, and which suggestions you added or dismissed.

Can I share a clip — with my doctor or dentist, say?

Yes: a clip of your own sleep talking, snoring, coughing or breathing. On the night's report, tap ••• next to the clip and choose Share this clip — or tap Share in Kept clips.

Moonsoon first tells you This audio will leave your phone. Nothing is sent until you choose Share the audio, and then only that one clip, as an audio file, to the app you pick. Nothing else from your night goes with it, and once it's sent, Moonsoon can't take it back.

You can share a clip you can play while its audio is still on your phone; a locked clip needs unlocking first. A clip you marked That's my partner — don't count it can't be shared — that voice isn't yours to send — and neither can room noise, movement, a baby or a pet. On a night you tracked your partner, the clips are theirs: ask them before you send one.

Seeing your doctor or dentist more than 7 nights from now? Keep the clip first, so it's still there.

Can I make a video of my night?

Yes — a short vertical video for Instagram, TikTok or a message: up to 4 moments of your own night, a few seconds of each real recording, on the night sky with the time and what was heard. On a night's report, tap the share button, then Make a 30-second video.

Before anything is made, you see every moment it will hold, and you can listen to each one or switch it off. With Show what you said on, your clearest words can appear in it too — never mumbling, and never words from a locked clip.

Only your own sounds go in: never a clip you marked That's my partner — don't count it, never anything heard while another app was playing audio through the speaker, and never a clip that's locked or whose audio is gone. A night you tracked your partner makes no video at all — its report shares a picture of their snoring instead.

It's made on your device, and Moonsoon never uploads it. Nothing is sent until you choose Share the video after This video will leave your device — and then only to the app you pick, with one line linking to Moonsoon. Moonsoon deletes its own copy as soon as the share sheet closes; once it's sent, Moonsoon can't take it back.

How long are my recordings kept?

The clips from your 7 most recent nights stay on your device. Older nights keep their score, timeline and numbers — and any words from Show what you said — but their audio is deleted and those clips show Audio expired. Clips you keep are the exception: they stay until you stop keeping them. Stop keeping one, and it's deleted like any other clip once its night is no longer one of your 7 most recent.

If recordings take up more than about 300 MB, older nights give up their audio sooner — never last night's, and never a clip you kept.

You can delete everything yourself at any time in Settings → Privacy → Delete all sleep data.

Can I keep a clip for longer?

Yes. On the night's report, tap ••• next to the clip and choose Keep this clip. A kept clip isn't deleted after 7 nights, or when recordings take up space: it stays on your device until you choose Stop keeping this clip, or delete your data.

You can keep any clip you can play; a locked clip needs unlocking first. A clip you marked That's my partner — don't count it can't be kept, and marking a kept clip that way stops keeping it.

You'll find them all in Settings → Your nights → Kept clips, newest first, to play, share or stop keeping.

The free plan keeps up to 3 clips at a time; Premium keeps as many as you like. If Premium ends, the clips you kept with it stay yours: they still play, any words read from them stay readable, and they don't count toward the free plan's 3.

Kept clips are stored compressed, so they take little space, and — unlike every other clip — they're part of your device's own backup (iCloud or your computer, if you back up): a new device restored from that backup has them too. Moonsoon itself never uploads them. A backup file from Settings → Your nights remembers which clips you kept but never carries their audio, so on another device restored from that file they show Audio expired.

Can I get my nights into a spreadsheet?

Yes. In Settings → Your nights, tap Export as a spreadsheet (CSV). You get one file with a row per night — date, bedtime and wake time, time in bed, time heard and time asleep, sleep score and snore score, minutes of snoring, snoring per hour, the share of the night spent snoring, breathing pauses, your tags, how rested you felt and whose night it was (yours or your partner's) — to open in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets. It's free on every plan.

Your notes go in only if you switch on Include your notes before the file is made. The file never has audio, the words from Show what you said, or anything from Apple Health: on nights with Apple Watch stages, the time asleep is Moonsoon's own measurement, not the Watch's. A tag you added from an Apple Health suggestion is your own, like any tag, so it's in the file. A blank breathing-pauses cell means that night had nothing to check. A night you tracked your partner has its sleep score and time asleep left blank.

Moonsoon shares it like a backup — save it to Files, AirDrop it or send it — and anyone who has the file can read it. A spreadsheet can't be restored: to move your nights to another phone, use Back up my nights.

How do I delete my data?

To delete one night, open its report, tap ••• at the top and choose Delete this night. The night, all its clips — the ones you kept too — and any text made from them are deleted for good, and Trends, your averages and experiments stop counting it.

To delete one recording, tap ••• next to the clip, then Delete this recording. Its audio and any text made from it are deleted; the sound itself still counts in that night's numbers.

To delete everything, go to Settings → Privacy → Delete all sleep data. It removes every night, all its clips and any text made from them, and it can't be undone.

Deleting the app removes everything Moonsoon stored, too. Nights already synced to Apple Health stay in Health — you can remove them in the Health app.

Your subscription isn't affected — it belongs to your Apple ID.

Subscription & billing

How much is Premium?

Premium is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year. Lifetime is a single payment of $99.99. These are the App Store's prices for your country.

Monthly and yearly plans renew automatically until you cancel. Lifetime never renews, and it includes every Premium feature added in the future.

I already paid. How do I get Premium back?

Moonsoon asks the App Store what your Apple ID owns each time it starts, so after reinstalling or on a new device, Premium or Lifetime usually comes back by itself.

If it doesn't, tap Restore purchases in Settings → Subscription — it's on the paywall, too. Make sure you're signed in to the App Store with the Apple ID you paid with.

Premium works on every iPhone and iPad signed in with that Apple ID. Your nights don't sync between devices on their own, but you can take them with you: Settings → Your nights → Back up my nights makes one file you keep somewhere private, and Restore from a backup on the new phone adds its nights. The file holds each night's numbers, notes and tags — no audio, nothing from Apple Health, and the words from your sleep talk only if you switch them on for that file. Setting up a new device from your old one's own backup (iCloud or your computer) brings your nights and the clips you kept, too.

How do I cancel or change my plan?

Subscriptions are handled by Apple. Tap Manage subscription below to change your plan or cancel — or go to the Settings app → your name → Subscriptions.

Cancel at least 24 hours before the renewal date to avoid the next charge. Premium stays on until the end of the period you've paid for; after that Moonsoon goes back to the free plan, and your nights stay on your phone.

Deleting the app doesn't cancel a subscription, and neither does buying Lifetime: after switching, cancel the subscription here too. Lifetime itself never renews, so it has nothing to cancel.

How do I ask for a refund?

Payments go through Apple, so refunds are requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com. Moonsoon never sees your payment details and can't issue refunds itself.

If something didn't work the way it should have, please tell us too — we'd like to fix it.

Troubleshooting

Moonsoon says it can't use the microphone

Moonsoon needs the microphone to hear your night. Turn it on in iOS Settings → Moonsoon → Microphone, then start the night again. Until it's on, Home says so above Start Sleep, with a way to the switch.

The microphone is only used while a night is being tracked, and the audio is analysed on your phone.

My night stopped early, has a gap, or its report is missing

If Moonsoon was closed during the night — swiped away, or stopped by iOS to free up memory — recording stops there. Moonsoon saves what it hears as it goes, so the next time you open it, it rebuilds the night up to that point and Home offers its report, as long as it lasted at least 20 minutes.

If something else takes the microphone in the night — a phone call, Siri, or another app that records — Moonsoon can't hear the room until it's free again. Either way, the night's report says so at the top, and time Moonsoon didn't hear never counts as quiet sleep: your sleep score and your snoring per hour count only the time it heard. A night it heard less than 60% of is left out of your 7-night average, your experiments and what changes your snoring.

To keep a night running: don't swipe Moonsoon away, keep your phone plugged in, and leave some free storage.

Does tracking drain my battery?

Listening all night uses some battery, so plug your phone in if you can. If you start a night at 30% or less without a charger, the session screen reminds you.

The bedside clock (the moon button during a night) keeps the screen on — turned down low, with your own brightness back when you leave it — which still uses more power: use it with the charger in, or simply lock your phone.

Charge it on the nightstand, never under a pillow or mattress.

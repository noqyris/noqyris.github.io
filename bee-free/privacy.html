Bee Free — Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Bee Free is a puzzle game made by Noqyris. This policy explains what the app collects, what it does not, and who else is involved.

It covers version 1.2 and version 1.3 of the game. They differ in when the questions about age and ads are asked, in what a "no" to ads means, in what a player under 16 gets, and in game statistics, which only 1.3 has. Where they differ, this page says which version it means. You can see your version in the game's Settings.

What Bee Free itself collects

The app has no account and no login. Your progress, stars, honey, power-ups, daily quests and settings are stored only on your device, and are deleted when you delete the app. If you turn on daily reminders, they are scheduled on your device by iOS; no server is involved and nothing is sent anywhere for them.

Version 1.2 sends us nothing. Since version 1.3 the game can send us game statistics about how it is played, but only after it knows your age, never if you are under 16, and in Europe only if you allow it. The section "Game statistics (Noqyris)" below says exactly what they are.

Advertising

Bee Free is free and shows ads served through Unity LevelPlay (formerly ironSource), which runs an auction between ad networks — Unity Ads and ironSource Exchange, both run by Unity Technologies. To show ads, tailor them, measure them and prevent fraud, Unity and the ad networks it works with may collect and process, as independent controllers:

  • your device's advertising identifier (Apple's IDFA — only if you allow tracking)
  • device and app information, such as device model, operating system version and language
  • approximate location, such as the country, derived from your IP address
  • ad data, such as which ads were shown and whether you interacted with them
  • diagnostic data about how the ad software is running

This data is handled under Unity's privacy policy.

Before any ads: your age, then your answer

In both versions no advertising software starts before Bee Free knows your age, and, if you are 16 or over, before you have answered the question about ads (version 1.2, and 1.3 in Europe) or Apple's tracking prompt (1.3 elsewhere); an answer you gave in version 1.2 counts. The age question is neutral: it offers every age and asks once on your device. What a player under 16 gets is under "Children".

Version 1.3

The first time you open the game, it shows a short welcome that links the Terms of Use and this policy, then asks how old you are. If you are 16 or over, what comes next depends on where your phone is, read from its time zone:

  • In the EU, the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Bee Free asks its own question about ads. It says who shows the ads, what data Unity receives and why, and has two equal answers, Accept and Decline. If you accept, Bee Free then asks for tracking permission through Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. If you decline, ads are still shown, but they are not personalised: Unity is told that you did not consent and not to sell or share your data, and Apple's prompt is not shown. Unity and the ad networks may still process the device, app, location and ad data listed under "Advertising" (the advertising identifier among them only while tracking is allowed for Bee Free in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking; if you allowed it earlier, declining here does not turn it off), to show and count ads and to prevent fraud.
  • Everywhere else (Serbia included), Bee Free asks no question of its own and shows no screen of its own before Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt. Ads are shown whichever you choose; only if you allow tracking may they use your advertising identifier and be tailored to you, and otherwise Unity is also told not to sell or share your data.

Then iOS asks once whether Bee Free may send you notifications (see "Reminders").

If you are updating from version 1.2, the game does not ask again what you already answered there. If you declined ads in 1.2, that answer stays: the ads you now see are not personalised, and the game tells you so once.

You can change your choice at any time in the game under Settings → Privacy & data → Privacy & ads: the "Personalised ads" switch everywhere (off, the ads you see are not personalised), and in Europe "Change my answer"; elsewhere it also leads to iOS Settings → Bee Free → Allow Tracking.

Version 1.2

Nothing is asked when you open the game, and no ads are shown before you have answered. The questions come on the way out of your first finished level, or earlier if you tap an offer to watch an ad for a reward: first how old you are, then, if you are 16 or over, Bee Free's own question about ads, in every country, with two equal answers, Accept and Decline.

  • If you accept, Bee Free asks for tracking permission through Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt, and then ads start. Only if you allow tracking may they use your advertising identifier.
  • If you decline, the advertising software does not start at all and no ads are shown. The offers to watch an ad for a reward are not shown either. The question comes back only when you tap such an offer yourself, and not sooner than 180 days after your no.

You can open the question again at any time under Settings → Privacy & ads.

Purchases

Bee Free offers optional in-app purchases: Remove Ads, honey packs, power-up packs, a limited-time Welcome Pack and the Honey Pot. Payment is handled entirely by Apple; the app never sees your payment details. What you bought is recorded on your device and read back from Apple, so Restore Purchases works on a new device.

Remove Ads stops banner and between-level ads for good. Optional reward videos — the ones you start yourself, such as "Watch ad for +3 moves" — stay available, so the advertising software may still run after the purchase (in version 1.2 only if you accepted ads), and Unity LevelPlay processes data as described above whenever it does.

Reminders

Reminders are local notifications about your daily gift and your streak, at most one a day for about a week after you last played. They are scheduled on your device by iOS; nothing is sent for them.

  • Version 1.3: iOS asks once, at the first launch, whether Bee Free may send notifications. "Allow" turns the reminders on.
  • Version 1.2: reminders are off until you turn them on; the game asks iOS for permission only when you tap "Remind me" or the Settings switch.

Turn them off at any time in the game's Settings or in iOS Settings → Notifications.

Game statistics (Noqyris)

This section is about version 1.3 and later; version 1.2 has no game statistics. The game sends us statistics about how it is played, so we can see where players get stuck or stop, what is too hard and what does not work. Only we (Noqyris) make and read these statistics; they are not used for advertising and we do not sell them.

When they are sent

  • Nothing is stored or sent until the game knows your age (the same question it asks before any ads, when you first open the game). Until then, what happens in the game waits in memory only and is dropped if you close the game.
  • Under 16: never. The game sends no statistics at all on that device, and the Settings switch for them is not shown.
  • 16 or over, in the EU, the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland: only after you tap "Allow" on the game's own "Game statistics" card, which has two equal answers and comes after the question about ads. Before "Allow" nothing is stored or sent.
  • 16 or over, elsewhere: on, until you turn them off in Settings.

What data

  • A random install number: a random ID the game creates on your phone. It is not the advertising identifier, not a device ID, and it is never given to anyone, including our ad network (Unity LevelPlay).
  • How you play: start and end of play and how long it lasted, how the game was opened (from its icon or from an event link) and whether it was started fresh or brought back from the background, where the previous session ended (screen, level, how long it lasted and whether it ended right after an ad), screens, levels and attempts with their result and time, the stars of a won level and whether extra moves (a revive) were used, tutorial steps, offers to watch an ad and whether you tapped them, ads loaded and shown, with the ad network, the ad unit's name and the revenue per ad, the error code when an ad fails to load or to show, rewards from reward videos, that the request to rate the game was shown, errors in the game and loading time.
  • Your answers: to the game's question about ads and to Apple's tracking prompt (accepted, declined or closed), and that game statistics were turned on.
  • Game version and build number, whether it is a test build, platform (iOS or Android), language, time-zone offset, the day the game was first opened on the phone and the number of play sessions on it.
  • Country: Cloudflare processes your IP address to deliver requests and limit their number; we do not store it and keep only the country derived from it.

We do not send your name, email, phone model, system version, advertising identifier, your age or purchases.

Legal basis

  • In the EU, EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland: your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)). The game asks you before anything is sent and stores or sends nothing until you choose "Allow".
  • In other countries: our legitimate interest in improving the game, and you can turn statistics off at any time.

Who processes the data

Cloudflare, Inc. processes the data for us under its data processing agreement. Individual events with the random install number are stored at Cloudflare, possibly outside the EU (USA), under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses. Daily totals without the install number are stored in the EU.

How long

  • individual events: 3 months, then deleted automatically;
  • daily totals (without the install number, cannot be linked to you): as long as the game exists;
  • the daily limit counter (a hashed form of the install number): 2 days.

How to turn it off and delete

In the game's Settings → Privacy & data, turn off "Game statistics": from then on the game sends nothing, also after a restart. "Delete my data" turns statistics off, deletes what statistics stored on your phone and asks our server to delete your data. The install number stays on your phone only until the server confirms the deletion. Once the server receives the request:

  • this install's data is no longer shown in the per-player view, right away;
  • the daily totals of the last 8 days are recalculated without it within one day;
  • older daily totals still include it, but only as part of a total (for example, one more player), without the install number;
  • individual events and the deletion mark (which contains the install number) are deleted automatically after 3 months.

If your phone is offline, the game asks again at every later start. Because we do not know who you are, we can find your statistics only through that button in the game; we cannot find or delete them by email. Your game progress stays.

Children

Bee Free is for players of all ages, children included. Since version 1.2, before anything to do with ads, the game asks once how old you are. Only the result, under 16 or 16 and over, is kept, on your device. The game sends your age to no one; in version 1.3 the advertising software is only told that a player under 16 is a child, as described below. What a player under 16 gets depends on the version.

Version 1.3: ads that are not personalised

If you are under 16, you see ads, but they are not personalised and the game never asks for tracking:

  • there is no question about ads, and the game never asks for tracking permission (Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt); if tracking was allowed for Bee Free in an earlier version, it stays allowed until you turn it off in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, and while it is allowed iOS still makes the advertising identifier available to the advertising software;
  • before the advertising software starts, Unity LevelPlay and the ad networks it works with are told that you are a child (the "child-directed" setting of the US children's privacy law, COPPA), that you have not consented to personalised ads, and that your data must not be sold or shared;
  • Unity and the ad networks may still process the device, app, location and ad data listed under "Advertising", to show and count ads and to prevent fraud; how Unity handles a child's data is in Unity's privacy policy;
  • Settings does not show "Privacy & ads" with its "Personalised ads" switch; "Report an ad" is there;
  • reward videos, purchases and Remove Ads work as for any player;
  • the game sends no game statistics at all, and the Settings switch for them is not shown.

If you told version 1.2 that you are under 16, the game does not ask again, and from version 1.3 that device shows ads in this way.

Version 1.2: no ads

If you are under 16, the advertising software never starts on that device: there is no question about ads, no tracking prompt, no ads and no offers to watch an ad, and no advertising data leaves the device. Remove Ads is not offered, because there are no ads to remove.

In both versions, purchases go through Apple, with its Ask to Buy and parental controls.

Your choices

  • Change your ad choice in the game: in version 1.3 Settings → Privacy & data → Privacy & ads ("Personalised ads", and in Europe "Change my answer"); in version 1.2 Settings → Privacy & ads.
  • Turn the game statistics off, or delete them (version 1.3): Settings → Privacy & data → Game statistics and Delete my data.
  • Report an ad that is inappropriate or not suitable for your age: Settings → Privacy & data → Report an ad (in version 1.2 Settings → Report an ad) opens an email to us, with the game's version filled in.
  • Turn off tracking at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • Buy Remove Ads to stop banner and between-level ads.
  • Delete the app to erase everything it has stored on your device.
